The Florida contractor exam is open book and multiple choice. You apply through Professional Testing, Inc. (PTI), then schedule at Pearson VUE once you are approved. A certified general contractor passes three parts: Business and Finance, Contract Administration and Project Management. Here are the 15 questions Floridians search most.

## Do you need a contractors license in Florida?

Yes, for most construction work. Florida splits contractors into certified and registered licenses. A certified license from the Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB) lets you work anywhere in the state. A registered license is tied to a local county or city certificate and only covers that area. General, building and residential contractors, plus trades like roofing, plumbing, air conditioning and pool work, all fall under the CILB. Electricians are licensed by a separate board, the Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board (ECLB). Handyman jobs that stay away from structural, electrical, plumbing and mechanical work can be exempt, but the line is narrow, so check with your county before you bid.

## Can I take the Florida contractors exam online?

No. The state exams are given in person at Pearson VUE test centers, not by remote proctoring at home. What you can do online is everything around the exam. You register and pay through the PTI website, you schedule your seat on the Pearson VUE site, and you can study with online courses, practice tests and an AI tutor. Plan the trip to the test center as part of exam day, and arrive early, because you will check in, store your bag and get your books inspected before you start.

## How do I schedule the Florida contractor exam?

There are two steps. First, apply with Professional Testing, Inc., which registers candidates and develops and scores the CILB exams. Once PTI approves your application, you can book your seat with Pearson VUE. The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) says you can schedule within 72 hours of PTI approval. Pick a date that leaves room to study each part, because you do not have to take all three parts on the same day.

## Is the Florida contractor exam open book?

Yes. DBPR states that all CILB exams are open book and made of multiple-choice questions. That sounds easier than it is. The books are thick, the clock is short and the questions are written so you have to find the exact page. People who fail an open-book exam usually knew the material but lost time flipping. Tab your books, highlight the tables you will need and practice looking things up under a timer before exam day.

## Which books are allowed on the Florida contractor exam?

Only the references on the official list for your exam part, and they have to be clean. Each exam has a published reference list in the Candidate Information Booklet. For Business and Finance the main book is the Florida Contractors Manual, along with other business and law references. The trade parts use code books, OSHA rules and construction references. Highlighting and permanent tabs are usually allowed, but loose notes and handwritten pages are not. Check the current booklet before you buy, since editions change and an old edition can cost you points.

| Exam part | Typical references |
|---|---|
| Business and Finance | Florida Contractors Manual, lien law, tax and labor guides |
| Contract Administration | AIA contract forms, Florida Building Code, OSHA |
| Project Management | Scheduling, estimating and safety references |

## How many questions are on the Florida general contractor exam?

Question counts depend on the part. Business and Finance has 120 questions over about 6.5 hours, and you need 70% to pass. Contract Administration and Project Management are separate parts with their own question counts and time limits, set out in the Candidate Information Booklet. Treat each part as its own exam with its own study plan. A score of 70% is the passing mark on every part, so you can pass one, fail another and only retake the one you missed.

## How hard is the Florida general contractor exam?

The Florida exam is hard mostly because of time and reading, not because the ideas are new. Experienced builders often struggle on Business and Finance, which tests lien law, payroll, taxes and accounting rather than field work. The trade parts reward people who can find a table fast. Pass rates are lower for first-time takers who skip timed practice. If you treat it as a lookup exam, tab your books and run several full practice tests, the difficulty drops a lot. Most people who fail say they ran out of time, not that they did not know the subject.

## How do you study for the Florida contractor exam?

Study the books you will carry in, not just a summary. A plan that works for most people looks like this:

- Get the exact reference list and current editions first.
- Tab and highlight the books so each chapter is one flip away.
- Take timed practice tests, one part at a time.
- Review every wrong answer and note which page had it.
- Repeat until you finish with time to spare.

An online course or AI tutor helps you see why an answer is right, and rental books that come pre-tabbed save hours. Give yourself several weeks per part rather than cramming all three at once.

## How do you pass the Florida contractor exam?

Pass by managing the clock. Answer the questions you know first, mark the ones that need a long lookup and come back. Never leave a question blank, since there is no penalty for a wrong answer. Use the index of each book instead of paging through chapters. Keep a short sheet of your tab names so you know where each topic lives. Most of all, practice under real time limits before test day, so the pace feels normal and you are not surprised by how fast 6.5 hours goes.

## How do I prove experience for a Florida contractor license?

You show four years of work in the category you want, and at least one of those years has to be as a foreman or supervisor. You can replace up to three years of that experience with college credit or military service. DBPR says any accredited college credit counts, not only construction courses. The experience has to be verified by someone who saw your work. Building officials can no longer vouch for you, so the person signing should be a certified Florida contractor, a licensed architect or a licensed engineer. Building and general contractor applicants also show experience in several specific types of work.

## How do you become a contractor in Florida with no experience?

You cannot get a certified license with zero experience, but you can shorten the path. College credit or military service can cover up to three of the four years, so the shortest route is three years of credit plus one year as a foreman. Another route is to work under a licensed contractor while you build hours, then sit for the exam once you qualify. Some people also start in a narrower specialty with lighter rules, then move up to building or general contractor later. Keep pay stubs, project lists and contact details for every supervisor along the way.

## How much is a contractors license in Florida?

Expect several separate costs rather than one fee. You pay PTI to register for the exam, Pearson VUE to sit each part, and DBPR for the license application once you pass. On top of that come books, a prep course, fingerprinting, insurance and a business license if you form a company. General and building contractors also need liability and property damage insurance before the license goes active. Check the current fee schedule on the DBPR site, since fees change, and budget for a retake just in case.

## What credit score do I need for a Florida contractor license?

A FICO score of 660 or higher meets the credit requirement. If your score is between 580 and 659, you can still qualify by passing a 14-hour financial responsibility course approved by the CILB and sending the certificate with your application. Below 580, you need to raise your score or meet other financial rules first. Pull your report early, since unpaid judgments or liens can hold up your application even with a good score.

## How do I check or renew a Florida contractor license?

Look up any license on the DBPR license search at myfloridalicense.com by name or license number. It shows the status, the category and the expiration date, which is how homeowners check a contractor before hiring. Certified licenses renew every two years by August 31 of even-numbered years, and you need 14 hours of board-approved continuing education each cycle. Registered licenses run on a different cycle. Finish your hours early so a late course does not stall your renewal.

## Can a Florida contractor work in another state, like Georgia or North Carolina?

Not on the Florida license alone. A Florida certified license covers Florida only. To work in Georgia or North Carolina you apply for that state's license, but you may not have to retake every exam. Florida accepts the NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of its own trade exam, and many other states accept NASCLA too. If you passed NASCLA, check each board's rules to see whether the score carries over. You will still need that state's business and law exam in most cases.
