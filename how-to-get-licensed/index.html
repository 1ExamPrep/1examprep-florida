How to get a Florida contractor license

To get a Florida contractor license, pick your CILB or ECLB license, register with Professional Testing, Inc., pass the open-book exams at Pearson VUE with 70% on each part, set up the business and apply to DBPR. In Florida you can pass the exams before you apply.

Key takeaways

  • A certified license from the state exams works anywhere in Florida; a registered license works only where the local certificate was issued.
  • Register with Professional Testing, Inc., then book each exam part at Pearson VUE.
  • Division I takes 3 parts and Division II takes 2; every part is open book and needs 70%.
  • Pass the exams first, then send DBPR one complete application with scores, credit report, experience and insurance.

Do you need a state license or a county one?

Florida has 2 kinds of contractor license. A certified license comes from the state exams and lets you work anywhere in Florida. A registered license rests on a county or city certificate of competency and is limited to that area. Journeyman electricians and plumbers are always licensed by the county. GITS and Prov county exams have their own pages.

Step 1: which license fits your work?

The CILB splits licenses into 2 divisions. General, Building and Residential contractors are Division I and pass 3 exam parts. Trades such as Air A, Roofing and Plumbing are Division II and pass 2. Electrical work goes to the ECLB, starting with Unlimited Electrical. This site covers 61 licenses.

Step 2: how do you register for the exams?

Register with Professional Testing, Inc. (PTI), which handles CILB exam registration for DBPR. After PTI approves you, schedule each part with Pearson VUE, which gives the exams on computer at testing centers across Florida. Electrical candidates apply to DBPR for the ECLB exam and then book with Pearson VUE.

Step 3: how do you pass every part?

Every part is open book, but only the references in the Candidate Information Booklet are allowed. The Florida Business and Finance exam has 120 questions over about 6.5 hours, open book, and you need 70% to pass. Every certified construction contractor takes it once; a current, active CILB licensee adding a category is exempt. Division I adds Contract Administration and Project Management. Tabbed, highlighted books and timed practice are what move scores past 70%. Start with Business and Finance prep, then your trade page.

  • Read each book's table of contents twice
  • Take at least 2 full timed practice exams
  • Aim to find any answer in under 3 minutes

Step 4: set up the business

The license is issued to you, and you qualify your company. Register the business with the Florida Division of Corporations, line up general liability insurance and workers' compensation or an exemption. A business setup service can handle the filing for you.

Step 5: apply to DBPR

Send the DBPR application with your passing scores, a credit report, proof of experience and insurance. Division I and Division II applications differ, so use the form for your license. The application service reviews the file before it goes in, which avoids the most common delay.

What delays a Florida license most often?

Most delays come from paperwork, not the exams. Applicants who plan for these 4 problems usually finish faster.

  1. An incomplete DBPR application, which stops review until the missing page arrives.
  2. Waiting to book Pearson VUE until the week you feel ready, when nearby seats may be taken.
  3. Bringing a book that is not in the Candidate Information Booklet, which the proctor will not allow.
  4. Studying only the trade part and skipping Business and Finance, which every certified contractor passes.

Frequently asked questions

Can I take the Florida contractor exam before I apply?

Yes. You register with Professional Testing, Inc. and pass the exams first, then apply to DBPR for the license with your scores.

How long are Florida exam scores good for?

Check your score report and the current DBPR rules, since the window to use passing scores can change. Most candidates apply within a few months of passing.

Do residential and commercial applicants take the same business exam?

Yes. The Florida Business and Finance exam has 120 questions over about 6.5 hours, open book, and you need 70% to pass. Every certified construction contractor takes it once; a current, active CILB licensee adding a category is exempt.

Does Florida accept NASCLA?

Yes, for the Division I trade exam. Since March 1, 2019 the CILB accepts NASCLA Commercial General Building scores, and you still pass Business and Finance and a Florida Building Code portion.

What is the difference between certified and registered?

A certified license comes from the state exams and lets you work anywhere in Florida. A registered license rests on a local certificate and is limited to the places that issued it.

Who is the qualifier?

The qualifier is the licensed person who takes responsibility for a business's contracting work. The license is issued to you, and you qualify the company.
