Getting a Florida state license takes 5 steps, and in Florida you can test before you apply. Pass the exams first, then send DBPR one complete application. The full walkthrough is on the licensing guide.

Frequently asked questions

Who issues contractor licenses in Florida?

The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) issues state licenses through the Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB) for construction trades and the Electrical Contractors' Licensing Board (ECLB) for electrical work. Counties issue journeyman cards and local certificates.

How many exams does a Florida contractor take?

Division I contractors (general, building and residential) pass 3 parts: Contract Administration, Project Management and Business and Finance. Division II trades pass 2: their trade exam and Business and Finance.

What is the Florida Business and Finance exam?

The Florida Business and Finance exam has 120 questions over about 6.5 hours, open book, and you need 70% to pass. Every certified construction contractor takes it once; a current, active CILB licensee adding a category is exempt.

Is the Florida contractor exam open book?

Yes. The CILB and ECLB exams are open book, but you may bring only the references listed in the Candidate Information Booklet for your exam.

Who gives the Florida contractor exams?

You register with Professional Testing, Inc. (PTI) and, once approved, schedule your seats with Pearson VUE, which gives the exams on computer.

Does Florida accept the NASCLA exam?

Yes. Since March 1, 2019 the CILB has accepted the NASCLA Commercial General Building exam in place of the Florida trade exam. Business and Finance and a Florida Building Code portion are still required.

Do I need Business and Finance for a second license?

No. A current, active CILB licensee who tests for a new category is exempt from Business and Finance under rule 61G4-16.0015 of the Florida Administrative Code.
